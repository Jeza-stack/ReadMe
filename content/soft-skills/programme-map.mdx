Employers consistently rank communication, teamwork, and self-management above technical grades — yet nobody timetables them. This programme gives them a structure: modules you work through deliberately, each with practice tasks, not just reading.

## What you will learn

How the programme fits together, where to start, and how each module builds toward a capability you can evidence in an interview.

## The guide

**The design principle: practise, then reflect.** Reading about communication changes nothing. Every module in this programme ends with a real-world practice task and a reflection prompt — the loop that turns knowledge into habit.

**The programme sequence:**

1. **Communication Essentials** *(available now)* — being understood: structure, audience, and the discipline of the main point first.
2. **Teamwork That Actually Works** *(available now)* — roles, friction, and the meeting behaviours that separate productive groups from painful ones.
3. **Time and Self-Management** *(in development)* — planning under pressure, the weekly review, and honest estimation.
4. **Presenting with Confidence** *(in development)* — structure, nerves, and delivery for tutorials and beyond.
5. **Professional Writing** *(in development)* — emails, reports, and asking for things in writing.
6. **Leadership for Beginners** *(in development)* — leading a group assignment without a title.

Start with Communication Essentials even if another module attracts you more — every later module builds on it.

**How to evidence these skills.** Keep a running note of concrete moments: *"led the group when our presenter dropped out"*, *"turned a 3-page email chain into one clear summary"*. Interviews reward specific stories, and this programme deliberately generates them.

## Why this matters for your studies

Group assignments, tutorials, and presentations are graded soft-skill performances — the same capabilities, assessed earlier. Improving them raises marks now and employability later.

## What next

Begin with Communication Essentials, then Teamwork That Actually Works. Log one evidence story this week.
