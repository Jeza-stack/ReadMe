Every semester students lose marks — sometimes degrees — not because they meant to cheat, but because they never learned where the AI line sits. This guide draws it clearly.

## What you will learn

Which AI uses are legitimate study, which are misconduct, and how to protect yourself when the rules are unclear.

## The guide

**The core principle.** Assessment exists to certify *your* capability. Any AI use that makes the submitted thinking someone else's — even a machine's — defeats that purpose and counts as misconduct, exactly like contract cheating.

**Generally safe (study uses):**

- Explaining concepts, generating practice questions, testing your own explanations
- Grammar and clarity feedback on writing that is already yours
- Brainstorming *before* you write, where you then select and develop ideas yourself

**Generally misconduct:**

- Submitting AI-generated text, code, or calculations as your own work
- Paraphrasing AI output to disguise its origin (this is still not your thinking)
- Using AI in closed-book or in-class assessments
- Inventing sources — if an AI gave you a citation you never opened, and you cited it, you fabricated a reference

**The grey zone — and the rule that resolves it.** Between the clear cases sit uses like AI-assisted outlining or heavy rewriting. The resolving rule: **check the assessment instructions, and if in doubt, ask the lecturer *before* submitting — then declare what you used.** A declared tool can cost you nothing; an undeclared one discovered later costs you the benefit of the doubt.

**Protect yourself with process evidence.** Keep your drafts, notes, and reading annotations. If your work is ever questioned, a visible trail of your own developing thinking is the strongest defence that exists.

**A note on AI detectors.** Detection tools produce both false positives and false negatives, so institutions increasingly rely on process evidence and oral defence instead. Do not assume "it passed the detector" means "it is acceptable" — the standard is honesty, not detectability.

## Why this matters for your studies

Integrity findings follow you: onto transcripts, into professional registration, across institutions. Five minutes of asking "is this use allowed?" is the cheapest insurance available to any student.
